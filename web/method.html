How this works

The model, what has been tried, and what was rejected — including the numbers, not just the conclusions.

The model

Each team carries an attack and a defence rating, fit from historical goals scored and conceded with home and away split out. Those ratings produce an expected goal count for each side, which becomes a probability for every scoreline, which adds up to the home / draw / away numbers shown on this site.

Recent matches count for more than old ones — a result from two seasons ago carries a fraction of the weight of one from last month. A Dixon-Coles correction adjusts the low-scoring scorelines, which independent Poisson models are known to get wrong.

BTTS and Over/Under 2.5 (see Markets) are derived from that same scoreline matrix — no separate model, no new data, and no change to what the 1X2 prediction says for any fixture.

Predictions are never overwritten. When the model changes, the new version is published alongside the old one rather than replacing it, so every fixture can show how each version called it. The headline accuracy figure only ever reflects one version at a time — averaging different predictors together would misrepresent both.

The model rarely predicts a draw. Draws happen in about a quarter of matches but are seldom the single most likely outcome, so the pick is usually home or away. Read the probabilities, not just the pick.

Backtest

Before anything was published, the model was replayed against 8 seasons of history — 3,040 predictions, refitting before every match date so no future data ever touched a fit. 54.0% accuracy, RPS 0.1999, against the market's 55.3% / 0.1953 — about a fifth of the gap to closing odds closed since the first version.

A backtest is not a track record: it is what the model would have done, not what it did in public. The numbers on Overview and Results are the ones that count. Full numbers, including two rejected experiments (an ELO ensemble, an injury-count signal — both flipped sign between validation windows and were not shipped) are in the project's README.

Known data incidents

2026-09-06 — Arsenal 2-1 Chelsea graded as a draw. TheSportsDB marked the match "finished" with a score of 1-1 just 45 minutes after kickoff — far too early for a genuine final whistle. The pipeline graded it on that information, correctly given what it was told: a draw, and an "Under 2.5" for the O/U market. The real final score, 2-1 to Arsenal, arrived in a later sync and is what the match record shows today.

The grade did not follow. Predictions are immutable once graded — the whole point of the record is that a result can never be quietly edited after the fact, including to fix a mistake that was not the model's. So this one match stays wrong on the record, permanently, and is marked with a ⚠ badge on Results and Markets rather than hidden or corrected.

For what it's worth: both model versions actually picked this match correctly (home win) and the Over 2.5 pick was also correct — both are locked in as misses because the grade compares against the wrong score. The BTTS "No" pick was a genuine miss either way, since both readings of the score have both teams scoring.

Fixed structurally, not just patched for this one match. Grading now requires a match's kickoff to be at least 150 minutes in the past — 90 minutes' play plus stoppage/extra time plus a safety margin — before a "finished" flag is trusted (MIN_GRADING_DELAY_MINUTES in prescore/config.py). A "finished" flag this premature will not be graded again.
